## "So, What Does DevRel Actually Do for Us?"

If you've worked in or around Developer Relations, you've heard that question. Maybe you've asked it! DevRel covers a lot of ground: content, docs, samples, events, community, product feedback, and more. It's hard to decide what to do first, hard to staff, and hard to explain to the people funding it.

I don't think that question is unfair. That's why my co-authors and I wrote [*Developer Relations Activity Patterns*](/developer-relations-activity-patterns/). The book covers 39 DevRel patterns, and each one includes how to measure whether it's working.

## What I Bring to a Team

On the developer experience side, I've:

* Built SDKs and reference applications for numerous programming languages
* Published reference documentation, guides, and tutorials
* Produced video tutorials and live streams, and conducted workshops
* Built custom integrations and automation tools, and contributed to production APIs and services
* Pushed for improvements on customers' behalf

On the leadership side, I've:

* Mentored developers, advocates, and cross-functional teammates
* Helped shape technical strategy for developer-facing projects
* Helped teams translate customer pain into product and platform improvements
* Created clarity across engineering, product, marketing, and community efforts

## The Illustrator Advantage

Here's something most developer advocates can't offer: I draw. A good drawing can explain a tricky concept faster than a page of text, and developers remember it (and share it) long after they've forgotten the slide it was on.

* **Visual explanations:** Like the [OAuth and OpenID Connect guide](https://developer.okta.com/blog/2019/10/21/illustrated-guide-to-oauth-and-oidc) above, I use drawings to walk developers through confusing technical topics.
* **Books:** I illustrated two books for Lars Klint, *Microsoft Azure in Action* and *Automate Everything, Notice Nothing*, the covers and a matching sticker set for Kyle Simpson's *You Don't Know JS Yet* (second edition), and the cover and chapter art for *Developer Relations Activity Patterns*.
* **Sketch notes:** I draw [sketch notes](/sketch-notes/) of conference talks and keynotes, and [Sketchnote Army featured me](https://sketchnotearmy.com/blog/2022/11/8/david-neal) as "the Geek Visualizer."
* **Events and swag:** event planning, sticker and t-shirt designs, and hosting for Pluralsight Link/ 2022 and 2023, plus [logo and swag designs for the Okta CLI](/content/images/portfolio/004-okta-cli-variations.jpg).
